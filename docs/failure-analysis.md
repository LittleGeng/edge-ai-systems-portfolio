# What did not work, and what I learned

Three original dataset records from August 4 are now stored locally. They agree on 432 episodes, 52,970 frames, and all 377 parquet hashes. The work on August 8-9 only synchronized and checked those existing files; no new experiments were run on those dates.

1. **The current SmolVLA path is much too slow for 30 Hz.** Steady throughput is 0.837 Hz. A queue can smooth timing variation, but it cannot make the policy produce new actions faster. In a 10-second replay, only 8 of 300 ticks received a fresh action and 284 used the safe fallback.
2. **The available telemetry is not enough for a defensible energy figure.** Tegrastats exposed several component rails but no confirmed total-module rail. I therefore left `energy_j_per_action` empty instead of adding values that may overlap.
3. **SmolVLA task success is still modest.** The main-condition success rate is 13.3%. This demonstrates an end-to-end experimental path, not production readiness.
4. **The Orin test covers only part of a robot system.** It uses synthetic inputs and does not include real sensors, actuators, network delay, or safety-loop processing.
5. **The compile result is encouraging but statistically inconclusive.** Paired LIBERO success was 20.0% for eager execution and 22.0% for compile, a +2.0-point difference with a 95% confidence interval of [-10.0%, +14.0%]. The point estimate is within the preset +/-5-point range, but the interval is not. Compile also added 1099 MB of peak RAM, triggered dynamic KV-cache recompilation warnings, and remained far from 30 Hz.
6. **Fewer EdgeDiffusion steps improved speed but destroyed task quality.** The 20, 10, 8, and 6-step settings reduced controller P50 to 217.6, 88.4, 80.4, and 65.2 ms, but all four produced 0% high-quality completion. On Orin, 6 steps met the 50 ms timing target while the quality-preserving 100-step setting did not. None of the tested settings satisfied both requirements.
7. **Changing the SmolVLA refill threshold did not solve the control problem.** On Orin, raising the threshold from 0.5 to 0.8 increased fresh-action ticks from 68.7% to 86.7%, but stale ticks also rose from 100 to 201. In latency-injected LIBERO runs, asynchronous success fell from 24% to 8% and from 40% to 4%.
8. **Direct 256x256 input resizing was not viable.** On the same 50 LIBERO episodes, success fell from 20.0% at 512x512 to 0.0% at 256x256, a -20.0-point difference with a confidence interval of [-32.0%, -10.0%]. A lower-resolution version would need retraining or distillation.
9. **A short 384x384 fine-tune did not recover enough quality.** The 6000-step, rank-32 seed-43 candidate reached 10.0% success versus 20.0% for the 512x512 reference. Because it stayed below the preset 15% continuation level, I did not spend the additional GPU time on three seeds and 20k steps.
10. **Targeted INT8 reduced weight storage but increased end-to-end action latency.** Weight-only and dynamic INT8 stayed within the chosen action-difference limits, yet first-action P50 rose from 1202.12 ms to 1415.67 and 5450.43 ms. This conclusion is specific to the tested TorchAO version, layer selection, model, input, and Jetson runtime.

For EdgeDiffusion, the next useful direction is a distilled or retrained low-step policy evaluated against the 100-step quality reference. For SmolVLA, profiling already shows that denoise VLM forward takes 60.60% of measured inference and prefix embedding takes 27.04%, so the next step should be kernel or export-level analysis of those stages before trying TensorRT, ONNX, or quantization-aware training.
